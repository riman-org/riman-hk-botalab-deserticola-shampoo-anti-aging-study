\begin{abstract}
Background: The global shift towards clean beauty has intensified demand for non-irritating, botanical anti-aging solutions for the scalp, particularly in humid subtropical climates like Hong Kong. Methods: A stratified survey of 200 consumers (N=200) evaluated the BOTALAB Deserticola Shampoo, focusing on its patented Deserticola/Chlorella Ferment (Loliolide), Inonotus Obliquus (Chaga), and Centella Asiatica formulation against traditional retinol-based products. Results: The product demonstrated exceptional sensory acceptance, with a Top-2-Box score of 95.5\% for lightweight texture suitability in HK's humidity. However, efficacy trust remains moderate; only 44.5\% strongly agreed that the Deserticola patent provides significant anti-aging benefits, mirroring skepticism toward Chaga (45.0\%) and Polyquaternium-10/Centella combinations (45.0\%). NPS is currently -1.0, indicating a passive majority (78.0\%) who are satisfied but not yet advocates. Cross-tabulation reveals that 'Efficacy Doubt' (100.0\%) and 'Brand Trust' (96.5\%) are the primary barriers to switching from retinol. Strategic Implications: To convert passives into promoters, RIMAN must leverage local clinical validation ('HK Local Testing', 67.5\% interest) and bundle deals (92.5\% interest) to mitigate price sensitivity among Middle Income segments (Mean 3.70).

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} BOTALAB Deserticola Shampoo, Scalp Anti-Aging, Retinol Alternative, Hong Kong Microclimate, Consumer Trust Metrics
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The BOTALAB Deserticola Shampoo utilizes a proprietary blend of Deserticola/Chlorella Ferment (Loliolide), Inonotus Obliquus (Chaga) Extract, Nettle Extract, Centella Asiatica, and Polyquaternium-10. Designed for the high-humidity environment of Hong Kong (76-80\% RH), the formulation prioritizes a lightweight, non-residue texture using Coco-Betaine and Lauryl Glucoside surfactants. The study employed a Likert-scale survey (N=200) across three major microclimates: New Territories, Kowloon, and Central. The methodology assessed sensory performance, perceived efficacy of botanical patents versus retinol, and willingness to switch based on demographic and psychographic (MBTI) variables.
\section{2. Quantitative Performance \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Quantitative data highlights superior physical performance in humid conditions. Question q01 (Lightweight Texture) achieved a mean of 4.18 and a Top-2-Box of 95.5\%, confirming strong adaptation to HK's subtropical climate. Scent profile (q02) scored 3.94 (91.0\% T2B), and foam density (q03) scored 3.98 (88.5\% T2B). Conversely, perceived efficacy of active ingredients lags behind sensory satisfaction. Trust in the Deserticola patent (q04) was 3.46 (44.5\% T2B), while Chaga antioxidant perception (q05) was 3.46 (45.0\% T2B). The combination of Polyquaternium-10 and Centella for thinning/sensitivity (q06) also hovered at 3.44 (45.0\% T2B). This discrepancy suggests that while users love the feel, they remain skeptical of the biological anti-aging claims compared to established retinol standards.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Segmentation analysis reveals distinct behavioral patterns. Affluent/High Net Worth individuals (Mean 3.90) and those with Doctorate/Clinical degrees (Mean 3.85) showed higher receptivity, likely due to greater education on botanical science. Conversely, Lower-Middle Income groups (Mean 3.59) exhibited lower composite scores, driven by price sensitivity (q07 Mean 3.85). Occupationally, B2B owners (Aesthetic Clinics, Mean 3.84) valued the formulation more than C-End creatives (Mean 3.60). MBTI data indicates that pragmatic types (ISTJ, ESTJ) had higher means (3.80-3.81) compared to intuitive-feeling types (INTP, INFP, \textasciitilde{}3.54-3.63), suggesting that logical, evidence-driven consumers are more open to this alternative if proven effective. Primary concerns driving purchase were Thinning Hair (42.0\%) and Dullness (38.0\%).
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Expansion Strategy}
The negative NPS (-1.0) signals a critical need for strategic intervention. The primary barriers to adoption are 'Efficacy Doubt' (100.0\%) and 'Brand Trust' (96.5\%), followed by 'Price Premium' (68.0\%). To address these, RIMAN should prioritize 'Clinically Proven' claims (95.0\% interest) and 'HK Local Testing' (67.5\% interest). Bundling strategies (92.5\% interest) can effectively counteract price resistance in the Middle-Income segment. Furthermore, positioning the product against 'Clinical Dermatology' benchmarks (74.5\% comparison rate) rather than mass market or luxury brands aligns with consumer expectations for scientific rigor. Future marketing must emphasize the safety of the 'Retinol-Free' claim (q08 Mean 3.42) through transparent, third-party verified clinical data to convert the large passive cohort into active promoters.

\section{Conclusion}
The BOTALAB Deserticola Shampoo excels in sensory adaptation to Hong Kong's humid microclimate, achieving near-universal approval for its lightweight texture (95.5\% T2B). However, commercial success is hindered by a significant gap between sensory satisfaction and trust in botanical anti-aging efficacy, resulting in a neutral-to-negative NPS (-1.0). Consumers, particularly in the Middle-Income and C-End segments, require robust clinical validation to overcome 'Efficacy Doubt' and 'Brand Trust' barriers. Strategic recommendations include leveraging local testing initiatives, bundling offers to mitigate price sensitivity, and reinforcing dermatological credibility. By addressing these psychological and economic friction points, RIMAN can transform the substantial passive user base into loyal advocates, securing a competitive position in the growing scalp-care anti-aging market.
